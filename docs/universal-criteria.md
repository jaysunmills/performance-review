# Universal Criteria

These three criteria apply to **all engineering roles** regardless of track, level, or function. Every team member is evaluated on these standards.

---

## 1. Craft/Quality

### Definition

Excellence in your core role responsibilities. This means consistently producing high-quality work that meets or exceeds the standards expected for your role and level.

### What "Craft" Means by Role

| Role | Craft Means |
|------|-------------|
| Software Engineer | Clean, tested, maintainable, performant code |
| Data Engineer | Reliable, efficient, well-documented data pipelines |
| ML/AI Engineer | Accurate models, reproducible experiments, production-ready systems |
| DevOps Engineer | Stable infrastructure, reliable CI/CD, clear runbooks |
| Security Engineer | Comprehensive threat coverage, secure implementations |
| QA Engineer | Thorough test coverage, effective automation, clear documentation |
| Engineering Manager | Effective team leadership, delivery excellence, talent development |

### Level 4: Meets Expectations

**Observable Behaviors:**
- Consistently delivers high-quality output without excessive rework
- Proactively considers edge cases, error handling, and failure modes
- Code/work is well-documented and maintainable by others
- Follows and improves team standards and best practices
- Catches own mistakes before review/handoff
- Takes pride in craftsmanship beyond "just making it work"

**Evidence Examples:**
- Code reviews with minimal revision requests
- Low defect rate in production
- Positive feedback from peers on code/work quality
- Documentation that others can follow independently

### Level 3: Needs Improvement

**Observable Behaviors:**
- Quality is acceptable but inconsistent
- Sometimes misses edge cases or error handling
- Requires frequent review feedback on the same issues
- Documentation is incomplete or unclear
- Meets minimum standards but doesn't exceed them

**Coaching Focus:**
- Establish personal quality checklist
- Pair with high-quality performers
- Review own work before submission
- Study team best practices

### Level 2: Below Expectations

**Observable Behaviors:**
- Frequent quality issues despite feedback
- Code/work requires significant rework
- Doesn't learn from past mistakes
- Impacts team velocity due to quality issues

### Level 1: Unacceptable

**Observable Behaviors:**
- Consistent quality failures
- Unable or unwilling to meet minimum standards
- Work cannot be shipped/used without major intervention

## 2. Speed

### Definition

Reasonable execution pace combined with proactive self-unblocking. This is NOT about working fast or long hours—it's about working effectively and asking for help when stuck.

### Key Insight

**The difference between a 4 and a 3 is often about asking for help.**

A 4 says: "I'm stuck on X. I've tried A and B. Can you help me think through other options?"

A 3 says: "I was stuck for two days but didn't want to bother anyone."

### Level 4: Meets Expectations

**Observable Behaviors:**
- Completes work within reasonable timeframes for complexity
- Asks for help when blocked (proactive, not reactive)
- Communicates progress and obstacles early
- Doesn't let blockers fester—escalates appropriately
- Manages own time effectively
- Provides realistic estimates and updates when at risk

**Evidence Examples:**
- "I tried X and Y, they didn't work because of Z. Can I get your input on other approaches?"
- Raises blockers in standup before they become critical
- Consistently meets commitments or communicates early when at risk
- Tracks and manages own work independently

### Level 3: Needs Improvement

**Observable Behaviors:**
- Does good work but takes longer than necessary
- Waits too long to ask for help
- "Didn't want to bother anyone" mentality
- Gets stuck without speaking up
- Misses deadlines due to unreported obstacles
- Needs to be "rescued" instead of asking for help

**Coaching Focus:**
- Normalize asking for help
- Set explicit check-in points
- Practice "help request" format
- Build psychological safety

### Level 2: Below Expectations

**Observable Behaviors:**
- Consistently slow execution without improvement
- Doesn't communicate delays until after deadlines
- Resistant to time management suggestions
- Impacts team commitments

### Level 1: Unacceptable

**Observable Behaviors:**
- Unable to complete work in any reasonable timeframe
- Complete lack of communication about status
- Severe impact on team delivery

## 3. Adaptiveness

### Definition

Willingness to change and flexibility in approach. This includes embracing feedback, adjusting to new information, and being open to different ways of solving problems.

### Why This Matters

Engineering is constantly evolving. Teams need members who can:
- Pivot when requirements change
- Embrace new technologies and approaches
- Learn from feedback and iterate
- Handle ambiguity without paralysis

### Level 4: Meets Expectations

**Observable Behaviors:**
- Embraces feedback and incorporates it quickly
- Adjusts approach based on new information
- Flexible when priorities shift
- Open to different solutions and perspectives
- Handles ambiguity constructively
- Learns new technologies/tools as needed
- Doesn't get defensive when plans change

**Evidence Examples:**
- "Good point, I hadn't considered that. Let me revise my approach."
- Quickly adopts new team practices
- Successfully pivots when project direction changes
- Proposes alternatives when original plan isn't working

### Level 3: Needs Improvement

**Observable Behaviors:**
- Slow to incorporate feedback
- Struggles with changes to plans or priorities
- "That's not how we did it at my last company"
- Attached to original solutions even when they're not working
- Needs excessive convincing to try new approaches
- Uncomfortable with ambiguity

**Coaching Focus:**
- Practice "yes, and" mindset
- Explore reasons for resistance
- Small experiments with new approaches
- Build comfort with ambiguity

### Level 2: Below Expectations

**Observable Behaviors:**
- Resistant to feedback
- Refuses to change approach despite evidence
- Negatively impacts team flexibility
- Creates friction when priorities shift

### Level 1: Unacceptable

**Observable Behaviors:**
- Actively resists necessary changes
- Defensive and combative when given feedback
- Unable to function in dynamic environment

## Scoring Summary

| Criterion | Level 4 | Level 3 | Level 2/1 |
|-----------|---------|---------|-----------|
| Craft/Quality | Consistently excellent, proactive about quality | Acceptable but inconsistent | Frequent issues, impacts team |
| Speed | Reasonable pace, asks for help when stuck | Good work but doesn't speak up when blocked | Very slow, no communication |
| Adaptiveness | Embraces change and feedback | Resistant but eventually adapts | Refuses to adapt |

## Using Universal Criteria

### For Self-Assessment

1. Rate yourself honestly on each criterion
2. Provide specific evidence for your ratings
3. Identify your biggest area for improvement
4. Create a development plan with your manager

### For Manager Evaluation

1. Rate each criterion based on observed behaviors
2. Document specific evidence and examples
3. Identify patterns over the review period
4. Prepare coaching recommendations for Level 3 areas

### For Calibration

1. Review evidence across your team
2. Ensure consistent application of standards
3. Challenge assumptions ("Would another manager rate this the same?")
4. Document calibration decisions for transparency
