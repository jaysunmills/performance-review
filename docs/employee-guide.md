## Welcome

This guide introduces our performance management system—a framework designed to help you understand exactly what success looks like in your role and how to achieve it.

**What makes this system different?**

Unlike traditional performance systems that force a bell curve or stack rank employees against each other, our system is built on a simple principle: **everyone can achieve the top rating**. There's no artificial limit on how many people can excel. If you meet the clearly defined standards, you earn the rating—period.

---

## How It Works

### The 1-4 Rating Scale

We use a straightforward four-point scale:

| Rating | What It Means |
|:------:|---------------|
| **4** | **Meets Expectations** — This is the target. You're delivering quality work at a reasonable pace and proactively getting yourself unblocked. |
| **3** | **Needs Improvement** — You're doing good work, but something is holding you back—often not speaking up when you're stuck. |
| **2** | **Below Expectations** — There are significant issues with quality, speed, or willingness to adapt that need immediate attention. |
| **1** | **Unacceptable** — Critical performance issues requiring urgent intervention. |

### The Goal: All 4s

To be eligible for merit increases and stock refreshers, you need to achieve a **4 in all areas**. This isn't about being perfect—it's about consistently meeting the standards we've defined for your role.

The good news? These standards are transparent, documented, and achievable. You know exactly what's expected.

## What You're Evaluated On

Every engineer is evaluated on two components:

### 1. Universal Criteria (Same for Everyone)

These three criteria apply to all engineering roles, regardless of your specific job or level:

<table>
<tr>
<td width="33%" valign="top">

#### Craft / Quality

*Excellence in your core responsibilities*

This means consistently producing high-quality work that meets the standards for your role. For a software engineer, that's clean, tested, maintainable code. For a data engineer, it's reliable, efficient pipelines.

**What a 4 looks like:**
- Your work is consistently high quality
- You catch issues before they reach review
- Others can easily understand and build on your work

</td>
<td width="33%" valign="top">

#### Speed

*Reasonable pace + proactive unblocking*

This is **not** about working fast or long hours. It's about working effectively and asking for help when you're stuck.

**What a 4 looks like:**
- You complete work in reasonable timeframes
- When blocked, you speak up: *"I've tried X and Y. Can you help me think through other options?"*
- You don't let problems fester

**What a 3 looks like:**
- *"I was stuck for two days but didn't want to bother anyone"*

</td>
<td width="33%" valign="top">

#### Adaptiveness

*Willingness to change and flexibility*

Engineering evolves constantly. We need people who can embrace feedback, adjust to new information, and try different approaches.

**What a 4 looks like:**
- You incorporate feedback quickly
- You're flexible when priorities shift
- You handle ambiguity constructively

**What a 3 looks like:**
- *"That's not how we did it at my last company"*

</td>
</tr>
</table>

### 2. Role-Specific Competencies

In addition to the universal criteria, you're evaluated on **2-4 competencies specific to your role and level**.

These competencies evolve as you progress:

| Level | Focus Areas |
|-------|-------------|
| **Junior** | Learning, executing defined tasks, asking good questions |
| **Mid-Level** | Independence, owning features, consistent quality |
| **Senior** | Technical leadership, mentoring, system thinking |
| **Staff** | Cross-team impact, architecture, strategic thinking |
| **Principal** | Org-wide influence, innovation, technical vision |

Some competencies only appear at higher levels. For example, **Mentoring** starts at the Senior level—we don't expect juniors to mentor others, but we do expect seniors to help develop junior team members.

Your specific competencies are documented in the rubric for your role. Each competency includes:
- A clear definition
- Observable behaviors for each rating level
- Suggestions for development

## The Key Insight

> **The difference between a 4 and a 3 is often about asking for help.**

A **4** says:
> *"I'm stuck on this API integration. I've tried mocking the responses and checking the auth tokens, but I'm still getting 403 errors. Can you help me think through what else might be wrong?"*

A **3** says:
> *"I was stuck on that for three days but didn't want to interrupt anyone."*

Asking for help isn't a sign of weakness—it's a sign of good judgment. Knowing when you're stuck and proactively seeking input is exactly what we want to see.

Each role has a detailed rubric that spells out:

- **What competencies apply to your level**
- **What behaviors demonstrate each rating**
- **How expectations evolve as you grow**

### Example: Senior Software Engineer (IC3)

| Competency | What a 4 Looks Like |
|------------|---------------------|
| **Code Quality** | Your code serves as an example for the team. You identify and pay down tech debt. You catch subtle issues in reviews. |
| **Technical Problem Solving** | You solve complex, ambiguous problems. You teach approaches to others. You anticipate problems before they occur. |
| **System Design** | You design scalable, reliable, maintainable systems. You consider operational concerns. You document decisions. |
| **Mentoring** | You actively mentor 1-2 junior engineers. You provide regular, actionable feedback. You create learning opportunities. |

## The Review Process

### Before Your Review

1. **Know your rubric** — Review the competencies for your role and level
2. **Gather evidence** — Document your accomplishments, challenges, and growth
3. **Complete your self-assessment** — Be honest about both strengths and areas for improvement
4. **Seek peer feedback** — Get input from people you've worked with

### During Your Review

- Discuss your self-assessment with your manager
- Review evidence together for each criterion and competency
- Identify development areas and create a plan
- Set goals for the next period

### After Your Review

- Work on your development areas
- Check in regularly with your manager
- Track your progress toward goals
- Don't wait for the next review to get feedback

## If You Get a 3

A rating of 3 means "Needs Improvement"—it's not a failure, it's an opportunity.

**What happens:**
- You and your manager create a focused development plan
- You get specific coaching on the improvement area
- You have regular check-ins to track progress
- You may receive a partial compensation increase

**Common reasons for a 3:**
- Not speaking up when blocked (the most common one!)
- Inconsistent quality
- Slow to incorporate feedback
- Limited collaboration with team

**The path to 4:**
- Identify the specific gap
- Create concrete action items
- Practice the new behaviors
- Get feedback along the way

## Frequently Asked Questions

### "Is this really not a curve?"

Really. If your whole team delivers great work, your whole team can get 4s. There's no quota.

### "What if I disagree with my rating?"

Talk to your manager. Ratings should be based on documented evidence and never be a surprise. If you have evidence that supports a different rating, present it. If you can't resolve it with your manager, you can escalate to their manager or HR.

### "How often are reviews?"

Formal reviews happen twice a year, but feedback should be continuous. Don't wait for a review to find out how you're doing.

### "Can I change roles or levels during a review period?"

Yes. If you change roles, you'll be evaluated against your new role's rubric for the portion of time you were in that role. Your manager will work with you on how to handle this fairly.

### "What about new hires?"

New hires typically have a ramp-up period. Expectations are adjusted for the first 3-6 months as you're learning the codebase, team practices, and domain.

## Getting Started

### 1. Find Your Rubric

Navigate to the rubrics folder and find your role:
- `rubrics/ic-track/[your-role]/` for Individual Contributors
- `rubrics/management-track/` for Engineering Managers
- `rubrics/tpm-pm-track/[your-role]/` for TPM/PM roles

### 2. Understand the Criteria

Read through:
- The universal criteria (Craft/Quality, Speed, Adaptiveness)
- Your role-specific competencies
- The behaviors for each rating level

### 3. Self-Assess

Think honestly about where you are today:
- Which areas are you strong in?
- Where do you need to improve?
- What evidence supports your assessment?

### 4. Talk to Your Manager

Share your self-assessment and discuss:
- Do they see you the same way?
- What should you focus on?
- What support do you need?
