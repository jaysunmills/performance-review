# Technical Project Manager Competency Rubric

This rubric defines the role-specific competencies for Technical Project Managers at each level. All TPMs are also evaluated on the three [Universal Criteria](../../../docs/universal-criteria.md): Craft/Quality, Speed, and Adaptiveness.

---

## Level Overview

| Level | Title | Experience | Key Competencies |
|-------|-------|------------|------------------|
| TPM1 | Junior Technical Project Manager | 0-2 years | Project Execution, Communication, Learning Agility |
| TPM2 | Technical Project Manager | 2-5 years | Project Execution, Risk Management, Stakeholder Management, Technical Understanding |
| TPM3 | Senior Technical Project Manager | 5-10 years | Project Leadership, Strategic Planning, Cross-Functional Leadership, Mentoring |
| TPM4 | Staff Technical Project Manager | 10+ years | Portfolio Management, Organizational Influence, Strategic Thinking, Executive Communication |

---

## TPM1: Junior Technical Project Manager

### Scope
Small project management. Focus on learning PM fundamentals.

### Competencies

#### Project Execution

**Definition**: Managing small projects from start to finish

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Creates clear project plans and timelines
- Tracks progress effectively
- Identifies issues early
- Follows PM methodology
- Delivers projects on time

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Plans lack detail
- Poor progress tracking
- Issues identified late

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- Projects fail to deliver
- No planning or tracking
- Cannot execute projects

**Development Suggestions:**
- Study PM methodologies
- Practice project planning
- Learn tracking tools
- Review project retrospectives

#### Communication

**Definition**: Communicating project status and issues clearly

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Provides clear status updates
- Documents decisions and outcomes
- Runs effective meetings
- Escalates appropriately
- Keeps stakeholders informed

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Updates lack clarity
- Poor meeting management
- Doesn't escalate timely

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- Poor communication causes issues
- Cannot run meetings
- Stakeholders surprised by issues

#### Learning Agility

**Definition**: Quickly learning PM skills and domain knowledge

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Learns PM tools and methods
- Understands technical domain
- Applies feedback from reviews
- Shows steady improvement

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Slow to learn PM skills
- Limited domain understanding

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- Cannot learn PM fundamentals

## TPM2: Technical Project Manager

### Scope
Medium project management. Expected to manage projects independently.

### Competencies

#### Project Execution

**Definition**: Managing medium-complexity projects successfully

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Manages multiple workstreams
- Creates detailed project plans
- Tracks dependencies effectively
- Delivers consistently on commitments
- Adapts plans based on changes

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Struggles with complexity
- Dependencies not managed
- Inconsistent delivery

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- Projects frequently fail
- Cannot manage at this complexity

#### Risk Management

**Definition**: Identifying and mitigating project risks

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Identifies risks proactively
- Creates mitigation plans
- Monitors and updates risk register
- Escalates appropriately
- Learns from realized risks

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Reactive to risks
- Poor mitigation planning
- Doesn't learn from issues

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- Major risks missed
- No risk management

**Development Suggestions:**
- Study risk management
- Create risk frameworks
- Practice risk identification
- Review project postmortems

#### Stakeholder Management

**Definition**: Managing stakeholder expectations and relationships

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Identifies all stakeholders
- Manages expectations effectively
- Builds strong relationships
- Handles difficult stakeholders
- Influences without authority

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Limited stakeholder engagement
- Expectations misaligned
- Cannot influence

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- Stakeholder relationships damaged
- Cannot manage expectations

#### Technical Understanding

**Definition**: Understanding technical context for project decisions

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Understands technical trade-offs
- Asks good technical questions
- Partners with engineering effectively
- Translates technical to business
- Knows when to defer to experts

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Limited technical understanding
- Cannot evaluate trade-offs

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- Makes poor decisions due to lack of understanding

---

## TPM3: Senior Technical Project Manager

### Scope
Complex project management. Expected to lead high-impact projects.

### Competencies

#### Project Leadership

**Definition**: Leading complex, high-impact projects

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Leads most complex projects
- Drives project strategy
- Makes critical project decisions
- Handles ambiguity well
- Delivers major initiatives successfully

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Struggles with complexity
- Cannot make critical decisions
- Poor ambiguity handling

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- Major projects fail
- Cannot lead at this level

#### Strategic Planning

**Definition**: Developing strategic approaches to projects

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Creates strategic project plans
- Aligns projects with business goals
- Identifies strategic risks
- Plans for contingencies
- Thinks beyond immediate project

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Limited strategic thinking
- Plans not aligned to business

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- No strategic approach

**Development Suggestions:**
- Study strategic planning
- Learn business context
- Practice strategic thinking
- Get executive mentorship

#### Cross-Functional Leadership

**Definition**: Leading across organizational boundaries

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Builds cross-functional relationships
- Drives alignment across teams
- Resolves cross-team conflicts
- Influences without authority
- Enables collaboration

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Limited cross-functional influence
- Cannot resolve conflicts

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- Creates cross-team friction

#### Mentoring

**Definition**: Developing junior project managers

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Actively mentors 1-2 TPMs
- Shares PM knowledge and skills
- Provides feedback on projects
- Helps mentees develop judgment
- Creates learning opportunities

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Doesn't proactively mentor
- Limited knowledge sharing

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- Avoids mentoring

---

## TPM4: Staff Technical Project Manager

### Scope
Portfolio-level project leadership. Expected to drive most critical initiatives.

### Competencies

#### Portfolio Management

**Definition**: Managing project portfolios and priorities

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Manages project portfolio
- Prioritizes initiatives strategically
- Allocates resources effectively
- Balances competing priorities
- Drives portfolio value

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Limited portfolio view
- Poor prioritization
- Cannot balance priorities

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- Portfolio mismanaged

#### Organizational Influence

**Definition**: Influencing organization-wide project practices

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Influences project practices org-wide
- Creates PM standards and frameworks
- Builds PM capabilities
- Develops PM community
- Represents PM function

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Limited organizational influence
- Doesn't improve practices

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- No organizational influence

#### Strategic Thinking

**Definition**: Connecting projects to business strategy

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Understands business strategy deeply
- Proposes strategic initiatives
- Identifies portfolio opportunities
- Thinks 1-2 years ahead
- Advises on strategic decisions

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Limited strategic view
- Projects disconnected from strategy

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- No strategic thinking

#### Executive Communication

**Definition**: Communicating effectively with executives

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Communicates to executives effectively
- Provides appropriate detail level
- Delivers difficult messages
- Influences executive decisions
- Represents projects well

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Poor executive communication
- Cannot influence executives

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- Damages trust with executives
