# Engineering Management Competency Rubric

This rubric defines the role-specific competencies for Engineering Managers at each level. All managers are also evaluated on the three [Universal Criteria](../../docs/universal-criteria.md): Craft/Quality, Speed, and Adaptiveness.

---

## Level Overview

| Level | Title | Team Size | Key Competencies |
|-------|-------|-----------|------------------|
| M1 | Engineering Manager | 4-8 engineers | People Management, Delivery Management, Technical Credibility, Team Development |
| M2 | Senior Engineering Manager | 8-12 engineers | People Management, Delivery Management, Technical Strategy, Organizational Development |
| M3 | Director of Engineering | 20-40 engineers | Organizational Leadership, Strategic Planning, Executive Communication, Talent Strategy |
| M4 | VP of Engineering | 40+ engineers | Executive Leadership, Business Strategy, Organizational Design, Industry Presence |

---

## M1: Engineering Manager

### Scope
Single team management. Expected to manage 4-8 engineers and deliver team commitments.

### Competencies

#### People Management

**Definition**: Effectively managing and developing direct reports

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Conducts effective 1:1s with clear agendas and follow-ups
- Provides regular, actionable feedback
- Writes clear, evidence-based performance reviews
- Addresses performance issues promptly and fairly
- Creates psychological safety for team
- Supports career development goals

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- 1:1s lack structure or consistency
- Feedback is infrequent or vague
- Avoids difficult conversations
- Reviews lack specific evidence

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- Poor team morale due to management issues
- Cannot give feedback effectively
- Team members leaving due to management

**Development Suggestions:**
- Study management frameworks
- Practice giving feedback
- Get feedback on your 1:1s
- Read management books (High Output Management, etc.)

#### Delivery Management

**Definition**: Ensuring team delivers on commitments reliably

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Team delivers commitments consistently
- Manages scope and expectations effectively
- Removes blockers proactively
- Communicates status and risks clearly
- Balances tech debt with feature work

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Occasional missed commitments
- Slow to identify or escalate risks
- Doesn't effectively remove blockers

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- Frequent missed commitments
- Poor communication on status
- Cannot manage delivery

#### Technical Credibility

**Definition**: Maintaining enough technical context to manage effectively

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Understands technical work well enough to ask good questions
- Can identify technical risks and trade-offs
- Participates meaningfully in technical discussions
- Knows when to defer to technical experts
- Stays current on relevant technologies

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Technical understanding too shallow
- Cannot evaluate technical trade-offs
- Over-indexes on technical work vs. management

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- Makes poor technical decisions
- Cannot understand team's work
- Lost technical credibility with team

#### Team Development

**Definition**: Growing the team's capabilities and skills

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Identifies skill gaps and addresses them
- Creates growth opportunities for team members
- Supports team members' career goals
- Builds team's technical and collaboration skills
- Creates succession plan for key roles

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Limited focus on team growth
- Doesn't create growth opportunities
- Doesn't know team's career goals

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- Team skills declining
- No career development for team
- Cannot develop team members

## M2: Senior Engineering Manager

### Scope
Multiple teams or larger team management. Expected to manage 8-12 engineers or multiple teams.

### Competencies

#### People Management

**Definition**: Managing larger scope with consistency and fairness

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Manages larger team or multiple teams effectively
- Ensures consistent management practices
- Develops team leads and senior engineers
- Handles complex people situations
- Creates inclusive team culture

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Inconsistent management across team
- Cannot develop leaders
- Struggles with scale

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- Poor team morale at scale
- Cannot manage at this scope

#### Delivery Management

**Definition**: Managing delivery across multiple teams or larger scope

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Drives cross-team coordination
- Manages dependencies effectively
- Handles escalations appropriately
- Delivers complex, multi-team projects
- Balances priorities across teams

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Poor cross-team coordination
- Cannot manage dependencies
- Escalates inappropriately

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- Major delivery failures
- Cannot manage at this scope

#### Technical Strategy

**Definition**: Influencing technical direction for teams

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Influences technical direction for teams
- Makes build vs. buy decisions
- Balances innovation with stability
- Addresses technical debt strategically
- Partners with staff+ engineers on strategy

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Limited technical influence
- Poor strategic technical decisions
- Doesn't partner with tech leads

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- Technical strategy causes problems
- Cannot influence technical direction

**Development Suggestions:**
- Study technical strategy
- Partner with senior ICs
- Learn about architecture trade-offs
- Attend technical decision meetings

#### Organizational Development

**Definition**: Building organizational capabilities and practices

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Improves team processes and practices
- Builds organizational capabilities
- Develops future managers and leaders
- Creates scalable systems for growth
- Influences org design and structure

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Limited focus on org development
- Doesn't build leaders
- Ad hoc processes

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- Organization not developing
- Cannot build capabilities

## M3: Director of Engineering

### Scope
Department management. Expected to manage managers and oversee 20-40 engineers.

### Competencies

#### Organizational Leadership

**Definition**: Leading large engineering organizations

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Manages engineering managers effectively
- Builds strong management team
- Creates organizational culture and values
- Makes tough organizational decisions
- Drives organizational change

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Struggles to manage managers
- Cannot drive organizational change
- Poor organizational decisions

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- Organization dysfunctional
- Cannot lead at this scope

#### Strategic Planning

**Definition**: Developing and executing strategic plans

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Creates compelling strategic plans
- Aligns team efforts with company strategy
- Sets appropriate goals and metrics
- Adjusts plans based on results
- Thinks 1-2 years ahead

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Plans lack clarity or ambition
- Poor execution on strategy
- Short-term thinking

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- No clear strategy
- Cannot plan strategically

**Development Suggestions:**
- Study strategic planning
- Learn OKR/goal setting frameworks
- Practice communicating strategy
- Get feedback from executives

#### Executive Communication

**Definition**: Communicating effectively with executives and board

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Communicates clearly and concisely to executives
- Provides appropriate level of detail
- Delivers difficult messages effectively
- Influences through communication
- Represents engineering perspective well

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Communication too detailed or too vague
- Struggles to influence
- Avoids difficult messages

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- Poor executive communication
- Cannot represent engineering

#### Talent Strategy

**Definition**: Building and maintaining a strong engineering team

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Develops talent strategy for organization
- Builds strong recruiting pipeline
- Retains top talent effectively
- Addresses talent gaps strategically
- Creates career frameworks and paths

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Limited talent strategy
- High turnover or poor hiring
- No career frameworks

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- Talent crisis
- Cannot build or retain team

---

## M4: VP of Engineering

### Scope
Organization-wide engineering leadership. Expected to manage directors and oversee 40+ engineers.

### Competencies

#### Executive Leadership

**Definition**: Leading engineering at the executive level

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Sets engineering vision and strategy
- Partners effectively with C-level executives
- Makes critical organizational decisions
- Represents engineering externally
- Builds world-class engineering organization

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Limited executive presence
- Cannot partner at C-level
- Poor strategic decisions

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- Engineering organization struggling
- Cannot lead at executive level

#### Business Strategy

**Definition**: Connecting engineering to business outcomes

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Understands business deeply
- Translates business needs to engineering strategy
- Drives engineering ROI
- Identifies technology opportunities for business
- Partners with product and business leaders

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Limited business understanding
- Engineering disconnected from business
- Cannot partner cross-functionally

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- Engineering misaligned with business
- Cannot understand business context

#### Organizational Design

**Definition**: Designing engineering organization for success

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Designs optimal organizational structure
- Builds appropriate teams and functions
- Creates scalable processes and systems
- Handles organizational complexity
- Plans for growth and change

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Organizational design issues
- Cannot scale organization
- Poor organizational decisions

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- Organization poorly designed
- Cannot handle organizational complexity

#### Industry Presence

**Definition**: Building company's engineering reputation externally

**Level 4 - Meets Expectations:**
- Represents company at industry events
- Builds employer brand for engineering
- Creates external technical content
- Develops industry relationships
- Attracts talent through reputation

**Level 3 - Needs Improvement:**
- Limited external presence
- Poor employer brand
- Doesn't represent company

**Level 2/1 - Below Expectations:**
- Damages company reputation
- Cannot represent externally
